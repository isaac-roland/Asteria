**The War of Wings**

**Pt. V**

Gerit and his men marched west; through hundreds of leagues of cliffs and forest. They needed to join forces with Lord Aldric, but even after leaving behind supplies and prisoners they were too slow. Lord Aldric Ravenstil and his son, Aldren, were met by the full force of The Crown. Neither side had yet heard about Gerit's victory. In the battle which later became known as the 'Bleeding Springs,' they found themselves forced into a fight with near 70,000 Princeps and Merek soldiers. Meanwhile, the Ravenstil-Blackstone army had approximately 55,000 men. This made for one of the largest battles in modern Shavian history. Leading the Princeps men was Hoster Lora, ancestor of Rickard Lora. He was a veteran of numerous battles against Illaneri incursions. On the other hand, Aldric Ravenstil was far less experienced. 

A common theme in this war was that the Princeps and allies had better overall leaders such as Mason Carthen and Hoster Lora, but struggled when it came to lower-ranking leadership and individual quality of their men. The Hearstons and allies (With the exception of Gerit) lacked experienced commanders higher up, but the quality of their men was much higher. The Hearston men had more to fight for than, say, a peasant from the Crownlands. Another example would be the Southron Knights of House Ravenstil or Blackstone being part of their tradition and identity. 

Aldric prepared his men for battle. He had two choices; Behind him was a stream, where Aldric could choose to retreat across. However, his knights would be almost across the water. But if he stayed where he was, the Princeps and Merek men could pin them against the river. Making a retreat impossible. Ultimately Aldric chose the latter option. Aldric gave command of the vanguard to his son, Aldren, which consisted of near 2,000 Ravenstil Knights. The rest of the Knights were put on either flank, mirroring the Princeps formation. The battle began when the vanguards clashed. In the open field the Ravenstil vanguard seemed to prevail; thousands of Hoster's men got trampled by the Ravenstil knights. After breaking the vanguard, Aldren made the mistake of continuing his push. Lord Hoster's center was ready. He put his strongest retinues at the front of the army. Arming them with pikes and spears. Aldren didn't know better; his men charged headfirst into a wall of spikes. 

Like rain upon rock, the vanguard was repelled. Horses were stabbed. Knights were impaled off of their horses. Men died left and right. Aldric concentrated his center to reinforce his son's failed attack. On the other hand, Lord Aldric used the traditional tactic of putting your inexperienced men in the front to protect your higher ranking soldiers. This meant that Lord Hoster was able to spread out his line to flank the Ravenstil and Blackstone men. 

Meanwhile, on the flanks, Lord Hoster's cavalry feigned retreat. They ran away from the battlefield, and led the Ravenstil-Blackstone knights over a hill where they were ambushed. Though Lord Aldric didn't know it yet, Hoster had not thrown his full force at the battle. The slugfest continued, and Aldric had managed to make progress. Hoster was slowly having to pull his men backwards. The heat of the afternoon had set in. Hours later there was a great sound in the distance. Hoster's cavalry was back. The ground beneath them shook. Thousands of knights raced towards the battle, under the banner of House Princeps. They slammed into the lines of their enemy. Lord Aldric's army had run short of morale. Hoster ordered his men to lock shields with the enemy and push. 

To put the final nail in the coffin, the tides began to rise. Men struggled to not be swept away in the river while the Princeps and Merek men push them with their shields. Chaos ensues. Men try to run across the river to retreat. On the opposite side of the river a small contingent of some Merek infantry and archers stands ready. They fire at anyone attempting to swim, and throw anyone who makes it across back into the water. Thousands drown, and even more bleed out. The battle ends when Lord Aldric is found dead, impaled by several arrows. His son was presumed dead, though later was found to have escaped capture by swimming away. Cheers from the Princeps and Merek men rang for miles, as they declared victory. 

The river was known as the Silver Rill flowed to a small lake named Silver Springs. Later the lake was renamed to Bleeding Springs when the blood turned it red for weeks to come.
